Three independent intellectual traditions --- modern quantum physics, the
process philosophy of Alfred North Whitehead, and the \bahai writings of
\Bahaullah and \AbdulBaha --- converge on the same structural
description of reality: that existence is constituted by relationship, and
that properties emerge only through relational interaction.
That love sustains those relationships is the claim of the \bahai writings
(\mahabbat) and, in his own vocabulary (\emph{Eros}), of Whitehead; physics
describes the structure but names no such principle.

This paper makes three related arguments.
First, local substance ontology is \emph{formally incompatible} with quantum mechanics:
Bell's theorem \citep{Bell1964}, combined with the experimental violation of
its inequalities \citep{Aspect1982,Hensen2015}, makes local realism untenable
and undermines the substance-ontological assumption that systems carry
intrinsic properties prior to measurement, making Whitehead's process ontology
arguably the most systematically developed internally consistent metaphysical
framework available for quantum reality.
Second, the \bahai writings constitute an \emph{independent prior instantiation}
of process ontology, articulating its essential claims decades before
\citet{Whitehead1929} named the framework philosophically and before
experiments on Bell's inequalities ruled out local realism.
Third, these two findings together support a \emph{triadic convergence}:
three traditions from different settings, using different methods across
170 years, describe the same relational structure.

The convergence is formalized through six structural isomorphisms (relational
ontology, nonlocal interconnection, observer-dependence, decoherence, holographic
order, and motion as life) and the Relational Coherence Framework (\RCF),
developed in a companion physics paper~\citep{Adams2026Physics}, which restates
standard decoherence theory in terms of a coherence quantifier, without
altering its predictions, and derives testable consequences for quantum-gate
scheduling.
The argument employs an overlapping consensus architecture: each tradition
validates its own thesis independently; the convergence is not a merged view
but independent evidence, from separate instruments, for the same underlying
structure of reality.
The convergence is three-way on relational structure and two-way on love;
that love is the principle sustaining the structure is the paper's central
interpretive claim, not a result of physics.
