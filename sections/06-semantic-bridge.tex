\section{The Semantic Bridge: One Structure, Many Vocabularies}
\label{sec:semantic}

The central interpretive claim of this paper is this:
\mahabbat is not a human emotion projected onto an indifferent universe.
It is the fundamental property of reality --- the force that forms and sustains
relationship at every scale --- which human beings experience, in its most
conscious and intense form, as emotion.
The claim belongs to the \bahai writings and, in his own vocabulary
(\emph{Eros}), to Whitehead.
Physics does not make it; what physics contributes is an independent
description of the relational structure the claim concerns.

Table~\ref{tab:semantic} presents the semantic bridge:
each domain's technical term, what it names, and the thesis it supports.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{The semantic bridge: one relational structure, four vocabularies.
Only the process and \bahai rows name a principle that sustains the
structure.}
\label{tab:semantic}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lXXl}
\toprule
\textbf{Domain} & \textbf{Technical Term} & \textbf{What It Names} & \textbf{Thesis} \\
\midrule
Quantum mechanics   & Entanglement
                    & A state of the whole not composed of states of its parts (structure only) & T1 \\
Process Philosophy  & Prehension / Eros
                    & Relational act of becoming (structure); creative drive toward richer relation (principle) & T2 \\
\bahai Writings     & \mahabbat / Affinity
                    & Creative foundation of all existence at every degree (principle) & T3 \\
Human experience    & Love
                    & The felt experience; on this paper's reading, the most conscious form of that principle & All \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The physics row names entanglement alone.
The Affinity Operator of the companion paper~\citep{Adams2026Physics} is
not a second name for it, despite sharing a word with the \bahai row:
its scalar summary $\bar\alpha$ is not a measure of entanglement.
It tracks a single system's local relational potential, the coherent
superposition that decoherence converts into correlation with the
environment, and it falls as that correlation grows
(Section~\ref{sec:rcf-phil}).
Entanglement names the correlation; $\bar\alpha$ tracks the potential spent
in forming it.

Note what is \emph{not} being claimed.
I am not asking physicists to call entanglement ``love,'' or \bahai scholars
to redefine \mahabbat as entanglement.
Each tradition keeps its own vocabulary, its own methodology, and its own
epistemic standards.
What I claim is that when each tradition's instruments point at the same
structure, from independent directions, across a 170-year sequence,
that agreement is evidence that the structure is real.

More precisely: the convergence is functional, not nominal.
What quantum mechanics describes as entanglement --- two systems that
have interacted, described by a single quantum state across spatial
separation and carrying a non-local coherence that belongs to the whole
and to neither part alone --- matches the structure the \bahai writings describe in terms
of \mahabbat: unified being held in coherent integration by the affinity
and agreement of its parts, such that the failure of affinity is
identified by \AbdulBaha as the very meaning of nonexistence.
Whiteheadian prehension describes the same structure in process
metaphysics: it carries the unity of prior occasions forward into the
present event of becoming, integrating a plurality of inheritances into a
single concrete actuality.
The argument is therefore not that three vocabularies refer to a single
substance under different names; that would be a category error and a
poetic conflation.
The argument is that three vocabularies converge on a single structural
feature --- integrated being whose unity belongs to the whole rather than
to its parts --- and identify it as load-bearing.
What the convergence detects is the structure; the names belong to the
vocabularies, the structure belongs to the world.
This is the move that structural realism, in its naturalized form
\citep{LadymanRoss2007}, makes generally: when independent inquiries
converge on a structural description, the structure has a stronger
claim to be real than any of the substantival posits used to picture it.
The present convergence makes that move concrete for one particular
structural feature: integrated, relationally constituted being.

Here the vocabularies part company.
The \bahai writings and Whitehead go beyond the structure and name what
sustains it: \mahabbat, the force that forms and sustains relationship,
and \emph{Eros}, the drive toward richer relation.
Physics names nothing of the kind.
Nothing in quantum theory sustains an entangled whole against separation:
an entangled system keeps its coherence only while it is isolated, and
coupling to an environment disperses that coherence into correlations
with the environment.
Physics registers the structure, not its value or direction.
The convergence on structure is therefore three-way and the convergence on
love two-way, and the identification of the sustaining principle with
love, the interpretive claim with which this section opened, rests on the
\bahai writings and on Whitehead, not on physics.

The table has a deliberate architecture.
Reading vertically, the four rows trace the same relational structure
across four domains of inquiry --- from the formal physics of
entanglement to philosophical reflection, revealed scripture, and
conscious experience.
Reading horizontally, each row gives the term native to its domain, and
the third column marks where the rows part: physics describes the
structure only, process philosophy and the \bahai writings also name a
principle that sustains it, and human experience is where, on this
paper's reading, that principle is felt as love.
No row is more fundamental than the others; entanglement is not
a more precise version of \mahabbat, any more than prehension is a more
precise version of love.
Each is the correct technical term in the vocabulary where its tradition
operates.

The deepest implication of the semantic bridge is not terminological
but ontological, though this final step is the most interpretively
exposed of the paper's claims and I flag it as such.
Physics cannot carry this step, since it names no sustaining force; the
inference runs through the other vocabularies.
If two traditions, working by different methods, name the same
principle --- Whitehead calls it \emph{Eros} in the philosophical vocabulary of
process, and the \bahai writings call it \mahabbat in the vocabulary of
revelation --- and if the structure they take it to sustain is the one
physics independently describes, then one candidate explanation, by
inference to the best explanation, is that the principle is real and
singular, that human beings know its most conscious form as love, and
that the diversity of names reflects the diversity of the instruments
trained upon it, not a diversity of things.
A weaker rival explanation deserves to be named rather than passed over:
that relational, holistic, or process-flavored metaphysics is common
currency across independent traditions, so that a structural match
this abstract is less surprising than a match on the far more specific
claim --- that the sustaining relational principle is best named
\emph{love} --- and that the three-way structural convergence
argued in \S\S\ref{sec:substance}--\ref{sec:convergences} is
doing more of the paper's evidential work than the two-way
identification of that principle drawn here.
I take the stronger reading to be the better explanation of the agreement
documented above, not a claim forced by the prior argument; a reader who
grants the structural convergence while declining the identification with
love has not thereby rejected anything essential to this paper's core
thesis.

This is not a new claim in the history of philosophy.
The intuition that love is a cosmic and not merely a personal force is
ancient (Empedocles' \emph{philia} and \emph{neikos} as cosmic principles;
Plato's \emph{Symposium}; Dante's ``love that moves the sun and other
stars'' at the close of the \emph{Paradiso}).
What this project adds is precision about the structure and a clearer
account of the evidence: a quantifier for the relational potential spent
in forming entanglement (the scalar affinity $\bar\alpha$), testable
consequences for scheduling the companion paper's two-qubit gate
($\URA$), and three independent lines of evidence that triangulate the
structure from different directions.
None of these tests the identification with love: physics can test the
structure, not the name.
The argument is not that love is important.
The argument is that love --- understood as the force of relational affinity
that constitutes and sustains existence at every scale --- is what reality
is made of.
The structural half of that argument has been reached by three traditions
that developed independently of one another.
The other half, that the principle sustaining the structure is love, is
shared with the \bahai writings and with Whitehead, and no experiment
decides it.
