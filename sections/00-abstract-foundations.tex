Three independent traditions --- quantum physics, Whitehead's process
philosophy, and the \bahai writings of \Bahaullah and \AbdulBaha --- converge
on the same structural description of reality: existence is constituted by
relationship, and properties emerge only through relational interaction.
That love sustains those relationships is a claim of the \bahai writings
(\mahabbat) and, in his own vocabulary (\emph{Eros}), of Whitehead; physics
names no such principle.

This paper makes three arguments. First, local substance ontology is
\emph{formally incompatible} with quantum mechanics: Bell's theorem and the
experimental violation of its
inequalities~\citep{Bell1964,Aspect1982,Hensen2015} undermine the
assumption that systems carry intrinsic properties prior to measurement,
making Whitehead's process ontology a systematically developed alternative
framework for quantum reality. Second, the \bahai writings constitute an
\emph{independent prior instantiation} of process ontology, articulating its
essential claims decades before \citet{Whitehead1929} and before experiments
on Bell's inequalities. Third, these findings support a \emph{triadic
convergence}: three traditions, three methodologies, across 170 years,
describing the same relational structure.

The convergence is formalized through six structural isomorphisms and the
Relational Coherence Framework (\RCF) of a companion physics
paper~\citep{Adams2026Physics}, which restates standard decoherence theory
in terms of a coherence quantifier, without altering its predictions, and
derives testable consequences for gate scheduling. Each
tradition validates its own thesis independently; the convergence is
independent evidence, from separate instruments, for the same underlying
structure. It is three-way on relational structure and two-way on love; that love
sustains the structure is the paper's central interpretive claim, not a
result of physics.
